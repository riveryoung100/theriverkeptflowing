## Doctrine and voice

The river is the continuing life/story. Trash is beliefs, identities, habits, conditioning, ambitions, behaviors, wounds and ego that entered the river but were never meant to define it. A river can keep flowing while carrying trash. The trash is not the river. Some was thrown in by culture, environment, relationships, music, circumstances or other people; some River willingly picked up and carried for years. Following God includes recognizing what does not belong, owning personal choices, removing it and continuing downstream.

> You are not pretending the trash was never part of your story. You are refusing to call the trash your identity.

Speak personally: “I believed this too. I wanted some of this. I participated in some of this. Some of it harmed me. Some of it harmed others. Some of it was handed to me. Some of it I picked up myself. God has been showing me what needs to leave the river.”

Examine beliefs rather than condemn people. Preserve responsibility without shame-content, superiority toward people who live differently, sanitized testimony, fake poverty aesthetics, prosperity-gospel promises or a spectacle of how terrible the past was. Continuation is the conclusion, not perfection.

Permanent public-language rule: avoid “Christian,” “Christianity” and religious identity-label language. Use following God, following Jesus, walking with God, faith or followers of Jesus as context requires. This rule applies to scripts, reflections, captions, quotes, titles, descriptions, metadata and derivatives.

## Flexible editorial arc

1. **What I believed:** state the old belief honestly, without sanitizing it.
2. **Where it came from:** culture, music, environment, hurt, insecurity, ambition, relationships, survival, ego or personal choice.
3. **What it produced:** effects on character, relationships, peace, priorities and self-understanding; name harm to others as well as oneself.
4. **What changed:** what God, experience, fatherhood, marriage, responsibility, failure, peace, wisdom or time revealed.
5. **What I believe now:** articulate the replacement belief, rather than only rejecting the old one.
6. **Throwing it out:** carrying something for years does not require carrying it downstream forever.
7. **The river keeps flowing:** continue with responsibility and hope, without claiming perfection.

This supports an installment; it is not a rigid script or required checklist of scenes.

Subject matter may include hood/street mentality; violence as strength; culturally or musically glorified killing; drill rap and music glorifying violence, drugs, revenge, sexual conquest, ego, money, disrespect or domination; drugs; sexual conquest and deliberately dishonored commitments; women/options as validation of manhood; fear mistaken for respect; ego disguised as confidence or “I'm the shit / nobody does it like me”; revenge; image/status; money for the wrong reasons; getting rich versus becoming wealthy; possessions as proof of worth; being used or taken advantage of; being needed confused with being valued; freedom confused with absence of responsibility; loyalty or gentleness mistaken for weakness; wanting the image more than the life; culture defining masculinity; and abundance pursued without stewardship. Treat these reflectively and personally, without sensationalizing them or assigning them to people as fixed identities.

Possible future concepts include “Violence Made Me Strong,” “I Thought Getting Rich Was the Goal,” “I Thought Having Women Made You a Man,” “I Thought Loyalty Was Weakness,” “I Wanted People to Fear Me,” “I Thought Drugs Made Life Bigger,” “I Thought Ego Was Confidence,” “I Thought Being Untouchable Meant Being Free,” “I Let Music Teach Me What a Man Was,” “I Wanted the Image More Than the Life,” “I Confused Being Used With Being Needed” and “I Thought More Money Would Fix Me.” These are examples only, not commissioned, recorded or published entries.

## Entry No. 001: planned manifesto

River physically carries household trash roughly half a mile to the dumpster. The ordinary task becomes the opening metaphor, without staging hardship as an aesthetic.

1. Pick up and carry the bags.
2. Let their weight introduce what River has carried internally.
3. Reflect on former beliefs and personal participation, using only what River chooses to truthfully tell.
4. Reach the dumpster.
5. Throw the trash away.
6. Walk home empty-handed. Preserve this return journey in the edit; it is essential visual language.
7. The same man continues on the same road without what he carried there.
8. The river keeps flowing.

Preserve the present financial truth: a gym membership is not financially sensible right now; carrying trash can literally be exercise, with room for River to joke that perhaps God put the workout in front of him. His hair is grown out because a haircut is not the priority. Do not cosmetically rewrite this season into fake success or imply he intends to stay here forever. Gratitude to Jesus belongs naturally in the piece.

Preserve River's belief that abundance is coming and is already being revealed differently, without guaranteeing wealth: “I believe abundance is coming and I am grateful for what God is already providing, but I am learning what abundance actually means before I try to display it.” Poverty is not proof of holiness; expected riches are not proof of God's favor. Distinguish abundance from performative wealth.

Useful contrasts: rich versus wealthy; feared versus respected; desired versus loved; options versus faithfulness; buying anything versus freedom; impressing people versus leading a family; possessions versus stewardship; status versus peace; appearance versus substance. These are reflection prompts, not claims of completed personal transformation or a finished script.
