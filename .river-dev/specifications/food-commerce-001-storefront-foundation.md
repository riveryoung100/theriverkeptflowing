## One descriptive source, existing food contracts

`src/lib/food/table-catalog.ts` is the **static conceptual presentation adapter**,
not a second live menu database or replacement food backend. The one current
concept uses existing `FoodRecipeDraft`, `FoodProductDraft`,
`FoodDishPresentation` and `FoodServingOffer` validation. IDs and exact versions
are stable within this local publication, not assertions of persisted records.
Future real records must be reconciled with the existing food authorities before
live use; no lookup or creation of remote records occurred in this mission.

The lifecycle covers DRAFT, IN_DEVELOPMENT, READY_FOR_REVIEW,
APPROVED_FOR_DISPLAY, AVAILABLE, PAUSED, SOLD_OUT and DISCONTINUED. Display mode
is separate: hidden, explicitly conceptual, or approved. Draft/retired records
are hidden. Available records need approved display; approved display needs
verified composition evidence and reviewed allergens. These are structural
assertions, not authenticated approvals. Ordering eligibility, exact SKU price,
capacity and handling information are checked separately. Neither a lifecycle
state nor a display approval enables checkout.

The adapter validates exact existing SKU/package/approved-price references when
present. `FoodPackageDefinitionVersion.netQuantity` supports future measured
portion grams. No proposed lab serving weights are published as final portions.
The current product has no SKU, package, price, final serving or nutrition data.
No empty categories or invented additional meals are rendered.

`publicTableProduct` constructs a whitelist projection, excludes hidden entries,
and removes recipe/product evidence, reviewer identity, private approval and
nutrition evidence references, and SKU/price approval internals. Components never
serialize raw source records or owner files into a client payload. Only approved
nutrition with explicit substantiation can project; none exists today. Complete
purchased-component subingredients are retained in the projected ingredient text.

For an owned photograph, change the catalog image to `owned-photo` with a local
`src`, meaningful `alt`, `caption`, `width` and `height`, then add the actual asset.
Use truthful captions and photographs of the real tested product. The same
component updates both routes. Remote URLs, traversal and missing dimensions are
rejected; no generated or stock meal photo is used.

## Calculation and fulfillment boundaries

`table-order-preview.ts` reuses `validateFoodPurchaseQuantity`, `validateFoodMoney`
and `createInitialFoodMerchandiseQuoteVersion` from the existing food domain.
The latter validates exact SKU/price matches and performs safe integer-cent line
and merchandise arithmetic. Only display-level fee/tax composition is added;
it does not replace `FoodPayableAssessmentVersion` for a real order.

The preview rejects invalid quantities, duplicate products, per-order/product/
fulfillment-capacity violations, unsafe money and overflow. Known fees use USD
integer cents and evidence; zero is an explicit verified amount, never a fallback.
Tax is explicitly unknown or externally verified with taxable/exempt/mixed
classification, exact amount and evidence. No rate or classification is computed
here. A verified exemption must carry zero tax. Any unresolved price, fee or tax
leaves the total unknown. Future tax evidence must cover the exact selected
products, fulfillment, jurisdiction and quote; this tool cannot establish that
provenance. All totals remain estimates, never payable authorizations.

`tableCommercialBlockers` checks availability, ordering/display/disclosures,
SKU/approved price, measured serving/handling information and known limits.
Fulfillment checks require eligible products, verified location/window/capacity,
delivery address verification and separate approval for out-of-area delivery.
No distance calculation, radius or fee schedule is invented. These input
assertions do not prove a held slot or verified address.

UI `pickup` maps to existing domain `meetup`; both delivery presentations map to
`personal-delivery`. These mappings do not create a real pickup location.
Out-of-area is a policy case requiring manual approval, not a new backend method.
No direct customer request is accepted today.

Every preview result contains literal `paymentAuthorized: false` and
`orderSubmissionEnabled: false`, even if every input is asserted verified. There
is no public path from a calculated estimate or readiness report to a processor.

## Private launch validator

`scripts/food/launch-readiness.mjs` separates display, production and commercial
review. Missing checks or PASS without evidence are UNVERIFIED. A recorded
negative decision is BLOCKED and dominates missing data. PASS means all required
assertions include references, not that software has verified the evidence.

Explicit launch authorization is its own exact-product/exact-version record,
requiring attribution, real date and evidence. A checkbox or complete ordinary
checklist cannot substitute for it. The report has no ordering switch. External
evidence authenticity, adequacy, freshness, physical safety and legal applicability
must be reviewed by responsible people. Keep all filled inputs under ignored
`.river-dev/state/`; never place owner data in `src` or `public`.

## Future secured integration path — not connected in this mission

Existing `/api/food/menu` uses server-configured publication identity/version,
the food database binding, read repositories and explicit public projection.
It remains unchanged and was not invoked. Future approved catalog data should
come through this authority rather than independently editing competing prices
or recipes in the storefront. The present menu projection is informational/
inquiry-only and lacks the entire checkout contract; do not assume a published
menu authorizes sale.

## Owner workflow

1. Recipe lab records actual repeatable recipe, yield, handling and reheating
   observations. Physical and qualified safety review produce evidence; software
   test success cannot approve a recipe.
2. Costing calculator takes receipt/yield/labor/three-part packaging costs and
   proposes a sustainable price. The the owner-defined target weekly operating-profit goal remains
   private; the public storefront contains no owner costs or profit target.
3. Private launch review records display, production, commercial and explicit
   launch authorization evidence for the exact product version.
4. Publish only selected approved customer facts through the existing domain
   authority and public projection. Keep draft claims, internal costs and private
   evidence outside public fields. Review the real photographs and disclosures.
5. A separately authorized future implementation must enforce server-side
   availability, holds, exact prices/taxes, authentication/authorization,
   idempotency, payments, confirmations and fulfillment. Only then consider
   enabling an actual limited launch.
