I have spent a lot of time imagining the moment when life would finally feel settled. There would be enough accomplished, enough built, enough secured. I would reach the place I had been working toward and discover that the longing had quieted down.

But reaching a goal has a way of revealing another goal. The place I once called arrival becomes the place from which I look toward something else. I can be grateful for what has changed and still feel that something remains unfinished.

I have been wondering how much frustration comes from asking this world to give me something it cannot hold permanently.

## Good gifts have limits

Work is good. Family is good. Friendship, rest, beauty, and the ability to create something useful are gifts. Remembering their limits does not require denying their goodness.

The trouble begins when I expect a gift to provide what only God can give. I ask an accomplishment to establish my worth. I ask a particular season to make me secure. I ask a possession to settle an ache that returns as soon as the possession becomes ordinary.

That places a weight on good things they were never meant to carry. It can also make me impatient with the very life I have been given. Instead of receiving it with gratitude, I judge it for failing to become permanent satisfaction.

## A beautiful world can still leave us longing

The world contains celebration and loss. Children grow. Seasons change. Work changes. Bodies age. Things we have cared for eventually wear out. Pretending those realities will not reach us cannot make our circumstances permanent.

In [1 John 2:15–17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+John+2%3A15-17&version=WEB), the warning against loving the world is connected with desires, pride, and what is passing away. That passage makes me examine what I am asking to become the center of my heart.

I do not want to turn that examination into contempt for life. The beauty of creation is still worth noticing. People are still worth loving deeply. Ordinary work is still worth doing well. But I want to receive those things without making them responsible for the deepest places in my soul.

The longing for something lasting does not have to mean I have failed to appreciate what is here. It can remind me to turn toward God rather than require another achievement to explain why I matter.

## Open hands change the way we live

Holding things loosely does not mean caring about them less. I can care for a home without treating it as the foundation of my identity. I can work diligently without believing that my worth rises and falls with the result. I can love people without pretending that a particular season with them will last forever.

In fact, remembering that a moment is a gift can make me more attentive to it. I do not have to postpone gratitude until I know how everything turns out. I can notice what has been entrusted to me today and respond with care.

Open hands also give disappointment a different place. Something can end without proving that every good thing it contained was an illusion. A plan can change without requiring me to rebuild my entire sense of worth around the next one.

I am still learning this. It is easier to say that God is my security than to stop organizing my heart around guarantees I cannot actually obtain.

## Receive the gift and return to the Giver

I do not want to withdraw from the world. I want to live in it more faithfully: grateful for its beauty, responsible toward its people, and honest about what it cannot promise.

That means asking what deserves my deepest affection. It means enjoying a blessing without expecting it to become a savior. It means allowing faith to shape the way I work, love, provide, and let go.

If I stop asking temporary things to become my permanent home, I may become freer to appreciate them as gifts. I can steward what is here without clinging to it as though nothing exists beyond it.

The world is beautiful. I want to remain grateful for that beauty. And I want my hope to rest in the God who gives it, rather than in my ability to make any part of it stay.
